Training methods are the techniques used to teach employees the knowledge and skills required for their jobs.

### [[A. On-the-Job Training Methods]]

**1. Coaching**

An experienced supervisor guides an employee and provides feedback during work.

Advantage: Provides individual guidance and immediate feedback.

**2. Job Rotation**

Employees move between different jobs or departments to gain broader experience.

Advantage: Develops multiple skills and improves understanding of the organization.

**3. Apprenticeship**

Employees learn through practical experience combined with structured instruction.

Advantage: Useful for technical and skilled occupations.

**4. Demonstration**

A trainer shows how to perform a task, and employees practise the task.

Advantage: Makes practical procedures easier to understand.

**5. Mentoring**

An experienced employee provides long-term guidance and career support to a less-experienced employee.

Advantage: Supports professional and personal development.

### [[B. Off-the-Job Training Methods]]

**1. Lectures**

A trainer explains concepts to a group of employees.

Advantage: Useful for communicating information to many people.

**2. Case Study**

Employees analyze a real or hypothetical business problem and suggest solutions.

Advantage: Develops analytical and decision-making skills.

**3. Role Playing**

Employees act out workplace situations to practise appropriate responses.

Advantage: Improves communication and conflict-management skills.

**4. Simulation**

Employees practise tasks in a controlled environment that resembles actual working conditions.

Advantage: Allows practice without exposing employees to the full risks of real work.

**5. Workshops and Seminars**

Employees participate in structured sessions to learn new concepts and exchange ideas.

Advantage: Encourages discussion and knowledge sharing.

**6. E-Learning**

Employees learn through online courses, videos, digital modules, and virtual classrooms.

Advantage: Offers flexibility in learning time and location.

### [[On-the-Job vs Off-the-Job Training]]

|On-the-Job Training|Off-the-Job Training|
|---|---|
|Conducted at the workplace while doing the job.|Conducted away from normal job duties.|
|Uses actual work situations.|Uses classrooms, simulations, or online learning.|
|Often involves practical experience.|May emphasize theory and structured practice.|
|Example: Coaching.|Example: Classroom lecture.|
