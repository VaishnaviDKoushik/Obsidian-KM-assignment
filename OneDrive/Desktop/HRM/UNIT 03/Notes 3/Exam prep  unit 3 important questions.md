# [[Part A: 2-Mark Questions]]

### 1. What is training?

Training is a systematic process of improving employees’ knowledge, skills and abilities to perform their present jobs effectively.

### 2. What is employee development?

Employee development is a long-term process of improving employees’ knowledge, skills and abilities for future responsibilities and career growth.

### 3. State any two objectives of training.

1. To improve employee performance.
2. To reduce mistakes and increase productivity.

### 4. What is Training Needs Assessment (TNA)?

Training Needs Assessment is the process of identifying the gap between employees’ existing skills and the skills required to perform their jobs.

### 5. What is on-the-job training?

On-the-job training is a method in which employees learn by performing actual work at their workplace under guidance.

### 6. What is off-the-job training?

Off-the-job training is provided away from the actual workplace through lectures, seminars, workshops and simulations.

### 7. What is induction training?

Induction training introduces newly appointed employees to the organization, its rules, policies, colleagues and job responsibilities.

### 8. What is performance appraisal?

Performance appraisal is the systematic evaluation of an employee’s job performance over a specific period.

### 9. What is mentoring?

Mentoring is a process in which an experienced person guides and supports a less experienced employee.

### 10. What is career development?

Career development is the continuous process of planning and improving an employee’s career opportunities and professional growth.

### 11. What is job rotation?

Job rotation involves moving employees between different jobs or departments to develop their skills and experience.

### 12. What is 360-degree feedback?

360-degree feedback is a performance evaluation method that collects feedback from supervisors, colleagues, subordinates and sometimes customers.

### 13. What is coaching?

Coaching is a method in which a supervisor or experienced person provides guidance to improve an employee’s specific skills and performance.

### 14. What is e-learning?

E-learning is a training method that uses electronic devices and digital platforms to deliver learning materials.

### 15. What is job enrichment?

Job enrichment means increasing the responsibility, independence and decision-making authority of an employee’s job.

# [[Part B: 5/6-Mark Questions]]

## 1. Explain the importance of training.

Training is important because it improves employees’ skills and helps an organization achieve its goals.

1. **Improves performance:** Employees perform their jobs more effectively.
2. **Increases productivity:** Trained employees can complete work efficiently.
3. **Reduces errors:** Training helps prevent mistakes and accidents.
4. **Builds confidence:** Employees feel more confident in their work.
5. **Improves quality:** Training helps maintain better standards.
6. **Supports new technology:** Employees learn to use new tools and systems.
7. **Increases job satisfaction:** Employees feel more capable and valued.

**Conclusion:** Training benefits both employees and the organization by improving efficiency and performance.

## 2. Differentiate between training and development.

|Training|Development|
|---|---|
|Focuses on the present job.|Focuses on future responsibilities.|
|Usually short-term.|Usually long-term.|
|Improves specific job skills.|Develops overall abilities.|
|Mainly job-oriented.|Mainly career-oriented.|
|Often provided to improve current performance.|Prepares employees for future roles.|

## 3. Explain the types of training.

1. **Induction training:** Introduces new employees to the organization.
2. **On-the-job training:** Employees learn while performing actual work.
3. **Off-the-job training:** Employees learn away from the actual workplace.
4. **Technical training:** Develops technical and job-specific skills.
5. **Safety training:** Teaches employees how to prevent workplace accidents.
6. **Refresher training:** Updates existing knowledge and skills.
7. **Soft-skills training:** Improves communication, teamwork and leadership.

## 4. Explain the methods of training.

### A. On-the-job methods

1. **Coaching:** A supervisor guides an employee.
2. **Job rotation:** Employees move between different jobs.
3. **Apprenticeship:** Employees learn a trade through practical work and instruction.
4. **Demonstration:** The trainer shows how to perform a task.
5. **Mentoring:** An experienced person provides long-term guidance.

### B. Off-the-job methods

1. **Lectures:** Information is delivered through presentations.
2. **Case studies:** Employees analyse real or hypothetical situations.
3. **Role-playing:** Employees practise workplace situations by acting out roles.
4. **Simulation:** Employees practise in a controlled, realistic environment.
5. **Workshops:** Participants learn through practical activities and discussion.
6. **E-learning:** Training is delivered through digital platforms.

## 5. Explain the steps in the training process.

1. **Identify training needs:** Find out which skills employees need to improve.
2. **Set objectives:** Decide what employees should learn.
3. **Design the programme:** Select content, methods, trainers and resources.
4. **Conduct training:** Deliver the planned training programme.
5. **Evaluate training:** Check whether learning objectives were achieved.
6. **Follow up:** Observe workplace performance and provide additional support.

**Conclusion:** A systematic training process helps ensure that training meets organizational and employee needs.

## 6. Explain the four levels of training evaluation.

The Kirkpatrick model evaluates training at four levels.

1. **Reaction:** Checks whether participants liked the training.
2. **Learning:** Measures the knowledge or skills gained.
3. **Behaviour:** Checks whether employees apply what they learned at work.
4. **Results:** Measures the impact on productivity, quality, costs or other organizational outcomes.

**Example:** After customer-service training, the organization collects feedback, conducts a test, observes employee behaviour and checks customer satisfaction.

## 7. Explain the objectives of performance appraisal.

1. To evaluate employee performance.
2. To identify strengths and weaknesses.
3. To provide feedback for improvement.
4. To identify training needs.
5. To support promotion and reward decisions.
6. To improve communication between managers and employees.
7. To set future performance goals.

## 8. Explain the methods of performance appraisal.

1. **Rating scale:** Employees are rated against specific performance criteria.
2. **Ranking method:** Employees are ranked according to their performance.
3. **Checklist method:** A list of statements is used to evaluate performance.
4. **Critical incident method:** Important examples of effective or ineffective behaviour are recorded.
5. **Management by Objectives (MBO):** Performance is assessed against agreed objectives.
6. **360-degree feedback:** Feedback is collected from multiple sources.
7. **Self-appraisal:** Employees evaluate their own performance.

## 9. Explain the importance of career development.

1. Helps employees plan their future careers.
2. Improves knowledge and professional skills.
3. Increases motivation and job satisfaction.
4. Prepares employees for promotion.
5. Helps retain talented employees.
6. Supports succession planning.
7. Benefits the organization through a skilled workforce.

## 10. Explain the methods of employee development.

1. **Coaching:** Provides individual guidance to improve performance.
2. **Mentoring:** Offers long-term professional support.
3. **Job rotation:** Provides experience in different jobs.
4. **Job enlargement:** Adds more tasks at a similar level of responsibility.
5. **Job enrichment:** Adds greater responsibility and decision-making authority.
6. **Special assignments:** Develops skills through challenging projects.
7. **Workshops and seminars:** Improve professional knowledge.
8. **Management development:** Prepares employees for leadership positions.
