Organizations provide different types of training according to job requirements and employee needs.

### [[1. Induction Training]]

Introduces newly appointed employees to the organization, policies, rules, colleagues, and job responsibilities.

Example: Explaining company policies to a new employee.

### [[2. On-the-Job Training]]

Employees learn while performing their actual jobs under the guidance of experienced employees or supervisors.

Example: A new employee learns to operate a machine under a supervisor's guidance.

### [[3. Off-the-Job Training]]

Employees receive training away from their normal work setting.

Example: Employees attend a classroom-based computer training programme.

### [[4. Technical Training]]

Develops job-specific technical knowledge and skills.

Example: Training programmers to use a new programming framework.

### [[5. Safety Training]]

Teaches employees how to work safely and prevent accidents.

Example: Training factory workers to use protective equipment.

### [[6. Refresher Training]]

Updates employees' existing knowledge and skills.

Example: Training employees on updated accounting rules or software features.

### [[7. Retraining]]

Provides employees with new or revised skills when their job requirements change.

Example: Teaching employees to operate newly installed machinery.

### [[8. Soft Skills Training]]

Improves communication, teamwork, leadership, and interpersonal skills.

Example: Conducting a communication skills workshop.

### [[9. Management Training]]

Develops planning, decision-making, leadership, and managerial skills.

Example: Training supervisors to manage teams effectively.

### [[10. Apprenticeship Training]]

Combines practical workplace experience with structured learning over a specified period.

Example: An apprentice learns electrical maintenance under qualified professionals.
