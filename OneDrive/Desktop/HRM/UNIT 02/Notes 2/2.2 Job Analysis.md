[[2.Job Analysis of the candidate:]]
### Meaning

Job analysis is the systematic process of collecting and studying information about a particular job, including its duties, responsibilities, skills, qualifications, and working conditions.

In simple words, job analysis explains what work an employee must perform and what is required to perform it successfully.
### [[Objectives of Job Analysis]]

1. **Identify Job Duties:** Determines the tasks an employee must perform.
2. **Determine Qualifications:** Identifies the education and experience required.
3. **Identify Skills:** Determines the technical and interpersonal skills needed.
4. **Support Recruitment:** Helps prepare suitable job advertisements.
5. **Support Selection:** Helps compare candidates with job requirements.
6. **Identify Training Needs:** Identifies the skills employees need to improve.
7. **Determine Compensation:** Provides information about job responsibilities and complexity.
8. **Improve Performance:** Clarifies employee duties and expectations.
9. **Avoid Duplication:** Reduces overlapping duties and unclear responsibilities.

### [[Information Collected During Job Analysis]]

- Job title and department
- Duties and responsibilities
- Authority and reporting relationships
- Educational qualifications
- Skills and knowledge
- Work experience
- Working hours and conditions
- Safety requirements

### [[Methods of Job Analysis]]

**1. Observation Method:** The HR professional observes employees performing their jobs.

- Advantage: Provides first-hand information.
- Limitation: Less suitable for complex mental and managerial work.

**2. Interview Method:** Employees and supervisors are asked questions about their jobs.

- Advantage: Provides detailed information.
- Limitation: Can be time-consuming and affected by personal opinions.

**3. Questionnaire Method:** Employees complete forms describing their duties and responsibilities.

- Advantage: Collects information from many employees.
- Limitation: Responses may be incomplete or misunderstood.

**4. Diary or Log Method:** Employees record their daily activities and the time spent on each task.

- Advantage: Shows the frequency and duration of activities.
- Limitation: Requires employees to maintain accurate records.

**5. Combination Method:** Two or more methods are combined to obtain reliable information.

### Example

Before recruiting a software developer, a company studies the duties, programming skills, qualifications, and experience required for the position.
