### Meaning

Employee separation refers to the ending of the employment relationship between an employee and an organization.

Separation may occur voluntarily or involuntarily, depending on the circumstances.

### [[Types of Employee Separation]]

**1. Resignation:** An employee voluntarily leaves the organization.

**2. Retirement:** An employee leaves employment after reaching the applicable retirement age or meeting retirement conditions.

**3. Termination:** The employer ends employment in accordance with the applicable contract, policies, and law.

**4. Layoff:** An employee is temporarily or otherwise released from work because of business conditions or other qualifying circumstances, as defined by applicable rules.

**5. Retrenchment:** Employment is terminated for specified operational or workforce-reduction reasons, subject to applicable law.

**6. Death:** Employment ends because of the employee's death.

**7. Expiry of Contract:** Employment ends when a fixed-term contract expires, subject to its terms and applicable law.

### [[Separation Process]]

1. Receive and document the resignation or separation decision.
2. Review applicable policies and legal requirements.
3. Communicate the effective date.
4. Arrange handover of responsibilities.
5. Recover organizational property and disable access when appropriate.
6. Calculate final salary and other eligible payments.
7. Complete required documents and records.
8. Conduct an exit interview where appropriate.
9. Complete the employee's formal exit.

### [[Exit Interview]]

An exit interview is a discussion with an employee who is leaving the organization.

Its objectives include:

- Understanding reasons for leaving.
- Identifying workplace problems.
- Collecting suggestions for improvement.
- Identifying retention issues.
- Improving HR policies and practices.

### [[Importance of Proper Separation]]

- Ensures orderly handover of work.
- Protects organizational information and property.
- Helps maintain accurate employee records.
- Supports compliance with employment requirements.
- Provides feedback for improving employee retention.
