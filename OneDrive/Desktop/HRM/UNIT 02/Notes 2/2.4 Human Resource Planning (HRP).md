### Meaning

Human Resource Planning is the systematic process of forecasting an organization's present and future employee requirements and planning how to meet them.

In simple words, HRP ensures that the right number of people with the right skills are available at the right time and place.

It is also called manpower planning or workforce planning.



[[Objectives of HRP]]

1. Ensure adequate availability of employees.
2. Avoid shortages and surpluses of employees.
3. Forecast future manpower requirements.
4. Use employees' skills effectively.
5. Support business expansion.
6. Identify future skill requirements.
7. Support recruitment and selection.
8. Plan training and development.
9. Support succession planning.
10. Control unnecessary labour costs.



### [[Importance of HRP]]

- Helps an organization prepare for future growth.
- Prevents delays caused by employee shortages.
- Reduces unnecessary staffing costs.
- Helps identify future training needs.
- Supports technological and organizational changes.
- Improves the utilization of available employees.

### [[Steps in Human Resource Planning]]

**Step 1: Analyze Organizational Objectives**

Understand the organization's future plans, such as opening branches or increasing production.

**Step 2: Forecast Human Resource Demand**

Estimate the number and types of employees required in the future.

**Step 3: Forecast Human Resource Supply**

Estimate how many suitable employees will be available internally and externally.

**Step 4: Identify the Gap**

Compare employee demand with employee supply.

- Demand greater than supply = shortage.
- Supply greater than demand = surplus.
- Demand equal to supply = balance.

**Step 5: Prepare an HR Plan**

Decide whether to recruit, train, promote, transfer, or redeploy employees.

**Step 6: Implement the Plan**

Carry out recruitment, training, and other staffing activities.

**Step 7: Monitor and Evaluate**

Review the plan and make changes when organizational needs change.


 [[Factors Affecting HRP]]

1. Organizational growth
2. Technological changes
3. Employee retirement
4. Resignation and turnover
5. Market demand
6. Government policies
7. Competition
8. Changes in organizational structure

### Example

A company planning to open two new branches estimates the number of managers, sales executives, accountants, and support staff required. It then decides how many employees can be transferred and how many must be recruited.
