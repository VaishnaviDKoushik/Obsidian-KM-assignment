### [[1. Trait Theory]]

Trait theory suggests that effective leaders possess certain personal qualities.

Examples:

- Confidence
- Intelligence
- Integrity
- Communication skills
- Emotional stability

**Limitation:** Having these qualities alone does not guarantee effective leadership in every situation.

### 2. [[Behavioural Theory]]

Behavioural theory focuses on what leaders do rather than only on their personal characteristics.

It commonly examines two broad behaviours:

- **Task-oriented behaviour:** Focuses on planning, instructions and completing work.
- **People-oriented behaviour:** Focuses on employee needs, relationships and support.

**Example:** A leader who sets deadlines demonstrates task-oriented behaviour, while a leader who listens to employee concerns demonstrates people-oriented behaviour.

### 3. [[Contingency Theory]]

Contingency theory states that leadership effectiveness depends on the situation. No single leadership style is best for every circumstance.

**Example:** A strict, directive approach may be suitable during an emergency, while a participative approach may be better for planning a new project.

### 4. [[Situational Leadership Theory]]

Situational leadership suggests that leaders should adjust their level of direction and support according to employees' competence and commitment.

**Example:** A beginner may need detailed instructions, while an experienced employee may need only general guidance.
