
Motivation theories explain why employees behave in particular ways and what encourages them to work effectively.

### [[1. Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs Theory]]

Abraham Maslow proposed that human needs can be arranged in five levels.

1. **Physiological needs:** Basic needs such as food, water and rest.
2. **Safety needs:** Job security, safe working conditions and protection.
3. **Social needs:** Friendship, belonging and teamwork.
4. **Esteem needs:** Respect, recognition and status.
5. **Self-actualization needs:** Opportunities to achieve one's full potential.

**Example:** An employee first needs a reasonable salary and job security. Later, recognition, responsibility and opportunities for personal growth may become important.

**Memory trick:** P-S-S-E-S  
Physiological → Safety → Social → Esteem → Self-actualization.

### [[2. Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory]]

Frederick Herzberg divided workplace factors into two categories.

**A. Hygiene factors**

These prevent dissatisfaction but do not necessarily create strong motivation by themselves.

Examples:

- Salary and benefits
- Job security
- Company policies
- Working conditions
- Relationship with supervisors

**B. Motivational factors**

These help create satisfaction and encourage employees to perform better.

Examples:

- Achievement
- Recognition
- Responsibility
- Promotion
- Opportunities for growth

**Example:** A fair salary may prevent dissatisfaction, while recognition for excellent work can increase motivation.

### [[3. McGregor's Theory X and Theory Y]]

Douglas McGregor proposed two different views of employees.

**Theory X**

- Employees generally dislike work.
- They may avoid responsibility.
- They may need close supervision.
- Managers may rely on strict rules and control.

[[Theory Y]]

- Employees can find work natural and meaningful.
- They can accept responsibility.
- They can exercise self-control.
- They can contribute ideas and creativity.

**Example:** A Theory X manager closely monitors every task, while a Theory Y manager gives employees suitable independence and responsibility.

### [[4. McClelland's Need Theory]]

David McClelland identified three important needs.

1. **Need for Achievement:** Desire to accomplish challenging goals.
2. **Need for Affiliation:** Desire to maintain friendly relationships.
3. **Need for Power:** Desire to influence or guide others.

**Example:** An employee who enjoys meeting difficult targets has a strong need for achievement.

### [[5. Vroom's Expectancy Theory]]

Victor Vroom stated that motivation depends on employees believing that their effort can lead to good performance and that good performance will lead to valued rewards.

The three important elements are:

- **Expectancy:** Effort can improve performance.
- **Instrumentality:** Good performance will lead to a reward.
- **Valence:** The reward is valuable to the employee.

**Example:** An employee works harder when they believe that extra effort will improve results and that good results will lead to a promotion they value.
