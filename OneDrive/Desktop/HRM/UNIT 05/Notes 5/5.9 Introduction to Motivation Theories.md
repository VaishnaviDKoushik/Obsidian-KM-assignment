Motivation theories explain the factors that encourage people to work, learn, and achieve goals.

### [[A. Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs Theory]]

Abraham Maslow organized human needs into five levels.

**1. Physiological Needs:** Basic needs such as food, water, rest, and adequate wages.

**2. Safety Needs:** Security, safe working conditions, and job stability.

**3. Social Needs:** Friendship, belonging, and supportive relationships at work.

**4. Esteem Needs:** Recognition, respect, achievement, and status.

**5. Self-Actualization Needs:** Opportunities to develop abilities and achieve personal potential.

**Order to remember:** Physiological → Safety → Social → Esteem → Self-Actualization.

**Application in HRM:**

- Provide fair wages.
    
- Ensure workplace safety.
    
- Encourage teamwork.
    
- Recognize achievements.
    
- Provide learning and career opportunities.
    

**Limitation:** People may pursue several needs at the same time, and the order of needs may vary across individuals and circumstances.

### [[B. Incentive Theory of Motivation]]

**Meaning:** Incentive theory suggests that people may be motivated to perform certain behaviours by rewards or incentives.

In the workplace, incentives can encourage employees to achieve targets and improve performance.

### [[Types of Incentives]]

**1. Financial Incentives**

- Bonuses.
    
- Commission.
    
- Performance-related pay.
    
- Profit-sharing.
    
- Salary increases.
    

**2. Non-Financial Incentives**

- Recognition and appreciation.
    
- Promotion opportunities.
    
- Flexible working arrangements.
    
- Awards and certificates.
    
- Training and career development.
    

### Advantages of Incentives

- Encourage employees to achieve targets.
    
- May improve productivity.
    
- Recognize good performance.
    
- Can increase employee motivation.
    
- Help attract and retain employees.
    

### Disadvantages of Incentives

- Employees may focus only on rewards.
    
- Poorly designed targets can encourage unhealthy competition.
    
- Incentives may be viewed as unfair.
    
- Some employees may value non-financial rewards more.
    
- Individual rewards can reduce teamwork if designed poorly.
    

### [[C. Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory]]

Frederick Herzberg identified two groups of workplace factors.

**Hygiene Factors:** Salary, job security, company policies, supervision, and working conditions. Poor hygiene factors can cause dissatisfaction.

**Motivational Factors:** Achievement, recognition, responsibility, advancement, and personal growth. These can increase job satisfaction and motivation.

**Application in HRM:** Organizations should provide fair working conditions and also offer meaningful work, recognition, and opportunities for growth.
