 ### [[A. Unitary Approach]]

The unitary approach considers the organization a single team with common goals.

**Features:**

- Management and employees share common interests.
    
- Cooperation is encouraged.
    
- Trade unions may be viewed as unnecessary by some unitary thinkers.
    
- Conflicts are seen as avoidable or caused by poor communication.
    

**Example:** Management and employees work together to improve productivity.

### [[B. Pluralist Approach]]

The pluralist approach recognizes that an organization contains different groups with different interests.

**Features:**

- Management and employees may have different goals.
    
- Trade unions play an important role.
    
- Negotiation helps resolve differences.
    
- Conflict is considered a normal part of working life.
    

**Example:** A trade union negotiates with management for better wages.

### [[C. Marxist Approach]]

The Marxist approach emphasizes differences in power and economic interests between employers and workers.

**Features:**

- Employers and workers may have conflicting economic interests.
    
- Workplace conflict is connected with inequality and control.
    
- Trade unions may help workers protect their interests.
    
- Wider economic and social structures influence industrial relations.
    

### Importance of These Approaches

These approaches help explain why workplace conflicts occur and how management, employees, and trade unions may respond to them.
